Working hypothesis: choosing an appealing fighter may invite more player interest than watching an elaborate collision. Meaningful short action/result text is worth testing; the data does not support a universal no-text rule. Keep the original Pick One unchanged as the current comparison point.

## Reserve use

The creator wants ready alternatives and is comfortable testing multiple ad creatives. There is no requirement to wait for one-at-a-time image production. A sensible next comparison includes the original Pick One, its lineup variant and at least one distinct action/outcome concept. The new social and CAST creatives add different motivations; the Kiln is a lower-priority collecting reserve because the earlier reveal concept was weak.

Suggested initial priorities: lineup variation, Perfect Release, You Win. Then 1v1 Me and Cast Now as different hooks, with Your Next Glass as the collecting alternative. These are editorial priorities, not predicted CTR rankings. Multiple creatives may run together; record comparable dates/settings and asset-level impressions, clicks, CTR, attributed plays, cost/play and subsequent engagement. Keep a reference point and avoid changing title, audience and artwork simultaneously if you want to interpret the difference. A 4–5% CTR remains the creator's target, not a guaranteed result.

## Name and emoji recommendation

Editorial judgment: **Glass vs Glass** communicates the competition clearly and works with both ordinary bottles and fantasy glassies. It is less distinctive as a phrase than Glass Clash, but clarity is useful for this current brief. No name-only test demonstrates a CTR lift, and this work is not a trademark/availability review.

Recommend keeping **Glass vs Glass** stable during the current creative comparison. The emoji is optional. If the creator wants one later, **Glass vs Glass ⚔️** is a reasonable single-suffix version: the name remains first and the symbol suggests a duel. There is no supplied evidence that this emoji improves CTR. No live name/emoji change was made.

[Roblox's naming guidance](https://create.roblox.com/docs/production/publishing/publish-games-and-places), checked 23 October 2026, recommends consistency, cautions against frequent renaming and says one or two well-placed emojis are acceptable while excessive decoration can confuse users. It does not promise an emoji-related performance lift.
