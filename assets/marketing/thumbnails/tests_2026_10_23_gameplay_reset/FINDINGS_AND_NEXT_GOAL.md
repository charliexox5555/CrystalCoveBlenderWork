# Glass Vs Glass — findings and next goal

Updated 23 October 2026. This is a reusable brief for future thumbnail work, based on creator feedback and supplied ad screenshots. The creator renamed the public game from **Glass Clash** to **Glass Vs Glass**. Historical files/source names remain Glass Clash; this art task did not rename runtime code or change the live game.

## The actual goal

Make a Roblox user understand the activity and want to try it in one glance. The creator's target is 4–5% ad CTR with economical player acquisition. Better-looking art alone is not the goal, and no CTR or cost result can be guaranteed. Measure actual plays and cost/play alongside clicks; do not increase spending simply because an image looks polished.

The immediate brief is a change in concept, not another change of creature, facial expression or energy trail within the same scene. Bright and exciting should come from an obvious action, choice or rivalry, with very little to decode.

## What the game really offers

Players collect glassies, receive three offers from their collection before a match, pick one, pull it back and release it against an opponent. Both fighters hang on strings and swing, collide, crack and eventually shatter. Match powers and optional CAST timing add variety; the match has three hearts. Glassies include ordinary bottles/jars as well as swords, dragons and other original fantasy shapes. The wooden arena and blue/red player sides are recognizable gameplay context.

An ordinary item can compete with a fancy one; do not advertise a guaranteed win from rarity. Do not invent handheld sword combat, glass cutting, a hammer-smashing minigame, giant-size upgrades or fake prize mechanics. A marketing selection graphic can summarize the real three-offer choice, but must not be described as an untouched screenshot.

## What we learned, and what is still a hypothesis

1. **The prior family has not delivered the desired CTR in this Plays test.** Bear Panic, Power Duel, Last Heart and Bottle Breaks Dragon differ in content but share glossy toy-like subjects, dramatic action, effect trails and arena framing. Swapping the creature again would not test a sufficiently different idea.
2. **Possible clarity problem, not a proven cause:** the cumulative ropes, wings, foreground faces, reaction avatars, shards, motion, beams and HUD can make viewers decode the image before recognizing a playable activity. The new title literally promises glass against glass; a simple recognizable object breaking or a clean matchup can explain that faster.
3. **Simple must not become lifeless.** The creator rejected calm product portraits and plain quiet gradients as boring. That was preference feedback, not measured evidence of low CTR for an uploaded campaign. Keep vivid color, scale contrast, a clear event or an active choice; avoid passive catalog presentation.
4. **Flash is not a standalone answer.** The creator likes bright, attention-grabbing references, but more bloom, a bigger shocked face or extra particles did not establish a successful direction. Keep any effect tied to the action. Competitor examples suggest hypotheses; their CTR is unknown and their assets are not ours to copy.
5. **Text is a variable, not a universal mistake.** Large branding took space in earlier images, but the old Unicorn's logo did not prevent its early lead. This round tests no text, two action words, and a compact VS. It cannot isolate typography from the other concept changes. Do not conclude that all text helps or hurts.
6. **Hands and native identity matter to this creator.** Repeated corrections concerned malformed C grips, human-looking fingers, misplaced wrists, chef arms and pasted avatar renders. When avatars are useful, use the saved bacon identities and correct classic block-ended arms. The current round intentionally needs no avatars or hands.
7. **Geometry still needs inspection.** Image generation repeatedly added extra strings and, on a Unicorn draft, an extra leg. One taut rope per glassy, correct attachment, recognizable source silhouette and plausible lighting are explicit checks. Do not call these AI illustrations actual screenshots or direct Blender renders.

## Next experiment and decisions

- Keep the public name, Plays objective and other campaign settings stable during the next comparison. Include one existing baseline, preferably Power Duel, with the three distinct challengers. Do not remove every reference point or treat historical Engagement CTR as the control for a Plays result.
- Record the same dates, audience/placement settings, creative identifier, impressions, clicks, displayed/calculated CTR, attributed plays and cost/play for each. Check click-to-play conversion and subsequent engagement too. A thumbnail that increases curiosity but attracts the wrong expectation can still be a poor acquisition creative.
- Select the strongest concept family only after comparable exposure; then make controlled revisions within that family. Avoid repeated restarts or declaring a winner after a handful of clicks. Learning status alone does not validate or invalidate a concept.
- No universal impression threshold, 4–5% benchmark or guaranteed result is established by the supplied evidence. If the new families also cluster near 1%, inspect the actual game/title/audience promise and a real gameplay-frame alternative before producing another near-identical render batch.
- Home personalization is a separate experiment. Do not apply Home-specific timing guidance as a claim about how Plays ads allocate traffic.

## Research and provenance

[Roblox thumbnail guidance](https://create.roblox.com/docs/production/publishing/thumbnails), checked 23 October 2026: use relevant imagery that communicates what users should expect, avoid ambiguous graphics, use a 16:9 format, and protect essential content from bottom metadata. This supports the clarity test, not a promise of specific CTR. Its video-specific restrictions should not be misquoted as a blanket prohibition on illustrated image thumbnails.

The developer notes pasted by the creator are useful direction about clarity, a reason to click, title alignment and delivering the promised experience. They are not an independently verified performance study or evidence that a particular art style works for all Roblox audiences.
