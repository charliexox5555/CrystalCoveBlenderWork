## 1. What Ball VS Ball is

Ball VS Ball is by the Roblox group **ATYS 3** (owned by the account AtysGames) and was created **Aug 12, 2026**. The listing reportedly calls it "auto-battle PvP in 1v1 and 2v2", with the tagline "Pick a ball. Watch it fight!"

The match loop, as fan guides describe it:

1. **Lobby.** You spawn on a server of up to about 25 players *(unverified, two sources agree)*. You walk around the lobby with WASD or the touch stick.
2. **Join a pad.** The lobby has match pads, each with a counter such as "0/2 PLAYERS". You stand on one and press E. A panel with Invite and Leave buttons appears and waits until a second player steps on. A short countdown follows.
3. **Choose your ball** (about 30 s, *single source*). The game offers **3 random balls from your own collection**. You can pay premium currency to reroll the three. The official listing reportedly also allows **one free pick of any ball you own per match**, but other guides call that option scarce or don't mention it. Guides say you may be able to see the opponent's ball before you commit *(low confidence)*.
4. **Adjust your aim** (about 30 s, *single source*). You drag a trajectory line and press **Lock Aim**. This is your only skill input. Guides advise banking shots off walls and corners instead of aiming straight at the enemy.
5. **Watch.** Both balls launch into a square arena with four walls. They ricochet, fire their abilities automatically and wear down each other's per-round HP bar. You have no further input. One wiki mentions mid-battle rerolls, which would contradict this; it is unresolved.
6. **Round ends.** When a ball reaches 0 HP, the **winning ball flies at the losing player and knocks off one of their 3 hearts**. Cosmetic "Flyers" change how that hit looks, and "Explosions" change the effect at the end of the round.
7. **Repeat** with a fresh draw of three balls. The match ends when one player has lost all 3 hearts, which makes it **first to 3 round wins and up to 5 rounds**. Several guides say "best of three", which doesn't fit the heart system.
8. **Results screen.** Coins (either 100 for a win and 20 for a loss, or based on performance; sources conflict) plus XP. Level-ups reportedly give a free ball. You return to the lobby.

**Skill versus luck:** the draw is random, and the same matchup doesn't always end the same way. Skill is in counter-picking (scout with a mid-tier ball in round 1, then counter what the opponent used last), in reading the opponent, and in angling your aim. One tier-list guide says ball choice "decides the match before it even starts."

**Unknowns that matter to us:** round time limit or sudden death, how 2v2 hearts work, the camera angle during fights, whether arenas sit next to lobby pads where people can watch (one source says it's fun to watch other matches, which suggests yes), how touch aiming works on phones, and whether there's a rematch button.

## 2. Content and economy

### Balls and rarities
- **Roster:** 33 balls in early September, about 42 by late September, "40+" on the official listing. It grows with updates.
- **Rarities:** Rare, Epic, Legendary and Mythic (one wiki lists only the first three). Per a tier list (opinion), **rarity does not equal strength**: Rare Spider and Vampire are rated S tier, while Legendary Chess and Verity are rated C.
- **Abilities fire automatically.** Examples: Vampire latches on and drains HP; Axe spins faster at low HP (a comeback mechanic); Charge hits harder the longer it waits; Cell grows, then splits; Conductor lays track after hitting a wall and a train rams enemies; Fibonacci's damage follows a growing sequence. **There is already a Legendary "Glass Ball"** that scatters shards on impact (about 4 damage each, *unverified*). That matters for our theme; see section 5.
- **Variants:** a "SHINY + RAINBOW" event (Oct 4–18) added **fusion**: 6 copies of a ball make 1 Shiny, and 6 Shinies make 1 Rainbow. Rainbows are serial-numbered. It's unknown whether variants change stats.

### How balls are obtained
| Route | Notes |
|---|---|
| Coin gacha | Price disputed: 150 coins (launch-era "Basic") vs 600 per pull or 6,000 for 10 (later sources). The launch had three 10-ball machines (Basic, Advanced 1, Advanced 2); these seem to have been replaced by a Coin gacha and a Diamond gacha (inference). |
| Diamond (premium) gacha | 35 Diamonds per pull or "68 Robux per pull"; the two figures don't reconcile with the reported pack prices. Has the "biggest tradeable pool including event balls". |
| Level-ups | Each level-up gives a free ball; the Level 3 reward is Verity (one wiki disputes free balls on every level). |
| Event Store | Limited-time balls bought with event currency. |
| Login bonus | Bomb Ball. |
| Robux Beginner Pack | Conductor Ball plus coins and spin tickets; guides call it "the most unfair P2W ball". |
| Robux limited bundles | Lightwing (Mythic) at 799 R alone or 1,599 R as a bundle, serial-numbered and automatically Rainbow; Shackles (Mythic) reportedly 1,200–1,599 R, "not in a gacha". |
| Trading | The official listing says yes; one wiki says no. |

The only odds found are **65% Rare / 30% Epic / 5% Legendary** for the now-removed Advanced 1 machine (Fandom). It's unknown whether the game shows odds in its UI.

### Currencies
- **Coins (free):** match payouts (100 for a win and 20 for a loss is the common claim); daily quests ("Play 10", "Win 3", "Duel a friend") worth about 100 each; playtime gifts worth up to about 400 per day plus a chance at a 10-minute 2x coin boost; codes UPD1–UPD8 at 100 each (some may have expired); one-time gacha pulls for your first battle, for liking the game plus joining the group, and for both players on a referral.
- **Gems / Diamonds (premium, Robux):** probably one currency with two names. Used for rerolling the 3-ball offer, the Diamond gacha, and a rotating daily shop of balls and cosmetics. Reported packs range from 100 Diamonds for 99 R to 13,000 for 9,999 R. One wiki claims a free source ("Special Milestones"); the rest say it is Robux-only.

### Every monetization point found
1. Gem/Diamond currency packs (dev products).
2. Paid rerolls of the 3-ball offer. This is **pay-to-win inside a match**.
3. Diamond gacha: a **paid random item**, odds disclosure unknown.
4. Daily rotating Diamond shop (balls and cosmetics).
5. Beginner Pack with an exclusive strong ball (pay-to-win).
6. Limited, serial-numbered Robux Mythics: scarcity and FOMO.
7. Cosmetic gachas (Explosions at 300 coins, Flyers at 600; whether these can also be bought with Robux is unknown).
8. Probably no gamepasses (Rolimons lists none), so revenue comes entirely from dev products.

Rolimons reportedly shows "spending" leaderboards *(low confidence)*.

## 3. Why it's working

### Evidence (tracker snapshots, unverified, approximate)
| When | Visits | Players online (CCU) |
|---|---|---|
| ~Sep 5 (3 weeks in) | ~3.3M | ~10K |
| Late Sep | ~42M | ~35K (peak ~48K) |
| Oct 10–12 | ~95–99M | ~47–60K; peak ~64–68K (Sun Oct 11) |
| Oct 13 | — | ~39K on a weekday afternoon, #34 on Roblox |

- About **88.7% like ratio** (241K up, 31K down), about 150K favorites, and **about 11.9 minutes average session** (Rolimons snippet).
- Visits roughly **doubled in the two weeks before Oct 11**. RoCodes labels it "trending downward", but that compares a Sunday peak with a Tuesday afternoon, so it is probably the normal weekly cycle and not a confirmed decline.
- **Update cadence:** about 8 code-numbered updates in about 8 weeks, each with a 100-coin code. The code-site ecosystem (PCGamesN, Pocket Tactics, Destructoid, Beebom and others) writes up every code, which works as free press. Code numbers may not map one-to-one to content updates.
- **Group funnel:** the ATYS 3 group grew from about 1.26M to about 4.19M members, driven by the free reward for joining. That gives the studio a large group to message for future games.
- **Experienced studio:** the same owner made **Bomb Chip** (Dec 2025), a simple 1v1 PvP game based on a viral challenge. It peaked at about 85.5K CCU on Jan 1, 2026, then **fell to about 1.4–2.7K**, a drop of about 97% *(unverified)*. The studio knows how to catch a viral spike and has also watched one collapse.
- **Creators and clips:** many TikTok matchup clips (one over 116K likes), tier lists and 2v2 combo videos. YouTube coverage is weakly attributed to SSundee and Caylus-linked channels.

### My reading of why it works
1. **It turns a format kids already watch into a game.** Earclacks' "Weapon Ball Battles" simulation videos (two balls in a box, guess who wins) went viral in late 2025. Ball VS Ball's link to them is *my inference*, not confirmed.
2. **Almost no skill floor.** Pick one ball, aim once, watch. That suits phones and young players.
3. **Short, self-contained rounds that clip well**, with a decisive finish (the hit on the losing player).
4. **Every match pays into a gacha.** Collection, rarities, fusion, trading and limited items give long-term goals.
5. **Drafting from your own collection** makes a bigger collection directly useful, so more pulls means more options in matches.
6. **Weekly updates plus codes** keep press coverage and returning players coming.

### Weaknesses and complaints
- **Pay-to-win:** Conductor (Beginner Pack) and Apple are each called "the most P2W ball"; paid rerolls and Robux-only Mythics add to it. Guides disagree on how bad it is.
- **Balance outliers:** Cell Ball is "literally cheating"; Dice Ball is "pure RNG".
- **Randomness:** physics outcomes vary and the draw is random, so losses can feel out of your hands.
- **Exploits:** about 63 scripts are listed on one exploit site, including "Auto Launch" and "Auto Win Duels".
- **Shallow input:** one aim per round. That helps reach and probably limits mastery and long-term retention.
- **Spike risk:** Bomb Chip's collapse is the studio's own precedent.
- **Kid-safety exposure:** paid random items with no confirmed odds display, serial-numbered scarcity, and spending leaderboards (if real).

## 4. The genre it belongs to

This section is mostly from model knowledge (cutoff June 2026); nothing was checked live.

| Comparable | What it shares |
|---|---|
| **Earclacks "Weapon Ball Battles" videos** (2025) and Roblox games openly inspired by them ("Weapon Ball Battles", "Spin Battles") | Same core image: things bounce in a box and one wins. Clones already exist, so the space is crowding. |
| **Blade Ball** (2023) | A ball-themed game with short rounds, spectating eliminated players and cosmetic crates. |
| **Rivals** (2024) | Short 1v1/2v2 competitive rounds plus cosmetic cases. |
| **Steal a Brainrot / Grow a Garden** (2025) | Collect-and-upgrade loops, restock timers, eggs/gacha, trading, live "admin abuse" events. |
| **Anime tower-defense gacha** (Anime Last Stand, Anime Vanguards) | Rerolls paid with premium currency (traits there, offers here). |
| **Bomb Chip** (same studio) | A viral real-world format made into a quick 1v1 lobby PvP game. |

**Patterns behind the genre's success:**
- Rounds of about 1–3 minutes with instant requeue.
- One input, so it plays well on mobile.
- A result that is readable at a glance but hard to predict, which makes clips worth sharing.
- Lobby servers where people waiting can watch live matches.
- Coins from every match feed a gacha you can pull often.
- Duplicates have a use (fusion or variants).
- New content plus a code every week.
- Free rewards for joining the group and liking the game.
- Collectibles that are distinct and meme-friendly, each with one obvious gimmick.

## 5. What this means for Glass Clash

### The short version
Glass Clash already has this genre's hook: **two collectibles collide and one breaks, and the server decides**. Our M1 prototype (server-authoritative 1v1, timing meter, cracks, slow-mo shatter) is closer to a "Ball VS Ball match round" than to a Pokémon route. The Pokémon structure (routes, trainers, catching, badges, evolution, repair) is the **most expensive part of our plan and the least proven** for this audience. Restructuring toward **short lobby PvP plus collection** is cheaper and faster, and it uses the strongest thing we've built.

This reverses **Decision 3** (Pokémon-like structure) and affects **Decisions 4, 5, 10 and 21**. It needs your explicit approval; nothing here changes DECISIONS.md by itself.

### Proposed core loop
```
Kilnport square (lobby, ~20–25 players, live stands you can watch)
  → step onto a free Clash Stand pad (1v1; a bot fills in after ~15 s)
  → Match: up to 5 rounds, first to break 3 of the opponent's Glass Hearts
       each round: draft 1 of 3 glassies from YOUR collection (~10 s)
                   → swing round: clashes with the timing meter until one shatters (~12–18 s)
                   → loser's Glass Heart cracks and pops
  → Results: coins + XP (win and loss both pay), quest progress
  → The Kiln (our gacha): spend coins to "blow" a new glassy (glassblowing reveal, odds shown)
  → duplicates → fuse for stars / Rainbow
  → back to the pads
```

### Match format
- **1v1, first to 3 round wins** (3 Glass Hearts each). Target **1.5–3 minutes** per match. Our current target is 4–6 clashes per fight (about 20–30 s). Retune to **3–4 clashes per round** (about 12–15 s), or a 5-round match drags. These numbers live in `Balance.luau`, so run the balance simulator and log the results.
- **Draft from your collection:** 3 random glassies from what you own, plus one **free "Pick any"** per match. **No paid rerolls.** At most, one free reroll per match earned by playing.
- **Hearts are glass, not avatars.** In Ball VS Ball the winning ball hits the losing *player*. For a glass game aimed at kids, don't throw glass at avatars. Each player instead has 3 small glass hearts (or ornaments) on their side of the stand, and one shatters with our existing shatter pipeline. That gives two shatters per round.
- **Bots are required.** A new game won't have Ball VS Ball's player count. If no human joins within about 15 s, use a bot from `Trainers.luau` (renamed "Rivals" or similar) through the existing `BattleLogic.npcChooser`. Our tournament design already plans bot-filled brackets.
- **2v2: postpone.** It's unclear how Ball VS Ball handles it, and it doubles the UI and network work.

### How our swing and shatter become the "fight"
- **Keep player input during the round.** This is our main mechanical difference: Ball VS Ball is aim once and watch, and Glass Clash is **you swing it yourself**. The timing meter is the "physical swing timing" that ties us to the real bottle videos. Make it forgiving (Weak by default if you do nothing, Perfect capped at x1.3, as now), so a kid who just watches still gets a fair fight and a skilled kid gets an edge.
- **Add Ball VS Ball-style "watchable" moments through powers.** `src/shared/Config/Powers.luau` already defines 15 powers (one passive that triggers on its own, plus one special the player taps once). It is **not wired into `BattleLogic` yet** and isn't in GAME_DESIGN. Wiring it in gives each glassy a visible gimmick (Fizz Burst, Sharp Edge bleed, Sturdy) the way Ball VS Ball's balls have one. This should be the main new combat work.
- **No physics.** The animated pendulum and server-decided results (Decision 7) stay. That also makes us harder to exploit than a physics game. Ball VS Ball's "Auto Win" scripts are a reminder to keep rate limits and Perfect-rate logging.
- **The shatter remains the reward** (Pillar 1). Each round ends in the slow-mo side-on shatter. Speed it up for rounds 1–4 (about 0.6 s of slow-mo) and save the full version for the match-winning blow, so 5-round matches don't drag.

### What to keep
| Keep | Why |
|---|---|
| `Formula`, `BattleLogic`, `Rng`, tests and balance simulator | The clash maths already is the round. It needs a round/heart wrapper and powers, not a rewrite. |
| `BattleService` / `BattleController` / `ShatterController`, cracks at 75/50/25%, compact HUD, `Theme`, UI components | The M1 feel work carries straight over. |
| Glassies roster (31 configured), **kitchen-to-fantasy** rarity, 3 types | The content plan already suits a collect-and-draft game, and the types give counter-pick depth like Ball VS Ball's matchups. |
| Starters Pop / Jam / Prism and the A-frame stand with octagon pen | **Give new players all three starters**, since a draft of 3 needs at least 3 glassies. |
| Shatter FX as the main cosmetic (Decision 8), Fills | This is our version of Explosions and Flyers, built on our own hook. |
| Kilnport map, **as a lobby** | The village square full of Clash Stands becomes the pad lobby; the furnace tower becomes **the Kiln** gacha landmark. |
| Rainbow variant and star fusion (GAME_DESIGN 4.2–4.3) | It already gives duplicates a use. |
| Lobby tournaments (8-slot bracket board) | **Later**, as a scheduled event. It is the real-life bracket format and a differentiator. |

### What to drop or postpone
| Item | Recommendation |
|---|---|
| Routes, Cullet Yard, zones and badges | **Postpone** (possibly forever). If you want solo content later, a "Rival ladder" of bot trainers in the lobby gives most of the value. |
| Wild glassies and Cork catching | **Drop for now.** The Kiln and level-up gifts replace catching. |
| Evolution at levels 15/35 | **Postpone.** Evolved forms (Rocket Pop, Prism Blade and so on) become separate collectible glassies instead of upgrades. |
| HP carry-over, Broken state, Repair Bench | **Drop.** Each round starts fresh. Decision 5 ("losing never deletes a glassy") still holds. |
| Per-glassy levels to 50 in PvP | **Drop for matches.** Use a flat match level, or stars only, so new players aren't crushed. Keep an account level for rewards. |
| Rarity as raw power (Mythic more than 2x Common) | **Change.** With a draft PvP game and Robux crates, rarity as power is pay-to-win. Make rarity mostly about **flair and power style**, with modest stat steps (Ball VS Ball's tier list suggests their rarity doesn't track strength either). This is a balance decision for you. |
| Pet following, starter pick screen | Postpone the pet; replace the starter pick with "you get all three". |

### Not a copy
- **Different hook and source:** real-life hanging glass-bottle bracket videos, not weapon-ball simulations. Lean into that recognizably: a wooden octagon pen, strings, a side-on slow-mo final shot, and a bracket board in the lobby.
- **Active input:** a swing-timing meter on every clash instead of aim once and watch.
- **The Kiln:** gacha as glassblowing in Kilnport's furnace, with molten glass forming into a glassy as the reveal. It fits the world and is ours.
- **Glass Hearts** in place of hitting the losing player.
- **Kitchen-to-fantasy collection**, which turns everyday objects into the chase.
- **Don't copy:** names ("Ball", "Flyers", "Explosions", "Gems/Diamonds"), the square ricochet arena, specific balls or abilities, their UI or art, serial-numbered paid scarcity, or "pick 1 of 3 + Lock Aim" screens copied wholesale. Learn the structure (lobby pads, draft from collection, hearts, coin gacha, weekly content) and build it in our own form.
- **Glass Ball overlap:** Ball VS Ball has a single "Glass Ball". It doesn't block a game where *everything* is glass, but it means "glass shattering" alone isn't unique. The swing format and the kitchen-to-fantasy collection are what make us different.

### Kid-safe monetization
| Do | Don't |
|---|---|
| Coin-only Kiln at launch; **show odds on the Kiln before every pull** | No paid rerolls or any Robux purchase inside a match |
| A Robux Kiln, if any, comes with odds before purchase and is **hidden when `PolicyService` → `ArePaidRandomItemsRestricted`**, with direct-buy alternatives offered | No Robux-exclusive glassies that are stronger (no "Conductor"); Robux-exclusive items are **cosmetic variants** only |
| Shatter FX, Fills, stand and heart skins (direct buy) | No serial-numbered limited paid items or countdown pressure aimed at kids |
| Gamepasses: VIP, 2x Coins, extra glassy slots (already in GAME_DESIGN 9) | No spending leaderboards |
| Free "Pick the Winner" while spectating | No betting or wagering of coins or Robux on matches, ever |
| Free group/like reward, daily quests, playtime gifts, update codes | Trading **postponed**: it brings scam, value and policy problems, plus moderation work |

**Risks:**
- The format is crowding (Earclacks clones, Ball VS Ball copycats), and spikes fade (Bomb Chip).

- Halloween (Jack-o'-Glass) is almost certainly too soon for this plan.

## 6. Questions for the user

1. **How far should we restructure?** Fully, so lobby PvP draft plus collection is *the* game and routes, trainers and catching are dropped (reverses Decision 3)? Or a hybrid that keeps a small solo "Rival ladder" against bot trainers?
2. **Input model:** keep the timing meter on every clash (my recommendation: active, skill-based, close to the real-life swing), go auto-battle after the pick like Ball VS Ball (more watchable, less skill), or one release per round?
3. **Rarity and power:** should rarity mostly mean *flair and power style*, with small stat steps (fairer PvP, less pay-to-win), or keep the current multipliers where a Mythic is more than 2x a Common?
4. **Kilnport map:** you're building it now. Do we cut it down to a lobby-only village square with stands plus the furnace tower Kiln, and shelve Cullet Yard and the harbor portals (affects Decision 21)?
5. **Monetization:** coin-only Kiln at launch, with any Robux Kiln later (odds plus PolicyService)? Or no paid random items at all and Robux only for direct-buy cosmetics and gamepasses? And are you OK postponing trading?
6. **Speed versus scope:** aim for a soft launch around Nov 10–16 with 12 glassies and 1v1 only? Or take longer and launch with tournaments and 2v2? (Also confirm whether "Glass Clash" and "glassies" stay as names.)
